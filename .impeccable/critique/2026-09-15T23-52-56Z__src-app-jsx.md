## Design Specificity Verdict

**Category-interchangeable. Fails.**

**LLM assessment:** A well-built generic developer portfolio wearing a freelance pitch as a hat. Swap `src/resumeData.js` for another engineer's and nothing about the composition would need to change. Five of the seven sections are the canonical dev-portfolio stack. The one section that exists because this is a client-acquisition surface (Services) sits in slot two, is absent from `NAV_LINKS`, and `.hero-scroll` links to `#about`, jumping the visitor straight past it. The nav proves who the site thinks it is for: About / Work / Experience / Skills are the four things a hiring manager wants; `#services` and `#contact` are unreachable from the nav at any width. One indigo ramp, one radius, one shadow and one hover lift are applied uniformly to six different card types across 9.3 screens with zero compositional rhythm. ## Overall Impression

The craft is real and the engineering is better than most production sites - the mobile menu's focus handling and the viewport-scoped motion are genuinely good work. But the page is built as a resume that had a services section bolted to the front, and the seams show in the order of the sections, the contents of the nav, and the fact that the one thing the site exists to produce - an email from a stranger - is reachable only by an unlabeled envelope icon disguised as a social link, or by a button at 94% scroll depth that opens an empty compose window.

The single biggest opportunity: six shipped apps with live App Store links is harder proof than any testimonial, and PRODUCT.md says testimonials will never exist. Right now that asset is a portfolio grid. It should be the design's central motif.

## What's Working

1. **The availability signal is honest persuasion, pulled twice.** The pulsing green dot paired with "Available for freelance & contract work" reads as live status rather than decoration, and the contact line closes the loop with a scarcity claim that is a binding fact rather than a manufactured one. On a surface with no testimonials, logos or metrics available to it, this is the one lever that is both available and pulled.

2. **The mobile menu is better than most production sites.** Escape closes, Tab cycles inside the overlay, focus moves in on open and returns to the toggle on close, and a matchMedia listener closes the menu if the viewport grows past the breakpoint that hides the toggle - so focus is never stranded in a menu with no dismiss control. Almost everyone ships that last case broken.

3. **Motion is scoped rather than ambient.** The IntersectionObserver sets `.is-idle`, pausing both drift glows, the status pulse, the conic portrait spin and the tagline interval the moment the hero leaves the viewport. Motion is treated as a property of a section being looked at, not as page-level texture.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The conversion path is a disguised icon and a button at 94% scroll depth, with no fallback
**Why it matters:** The success metric is one thing - a stranger emails. The page has exactly two mailto links: an unlabeled 42px envelope sitting between LinkedIn and GitHub, so it reads as a social profile rather than the conversion; and the contact button at y=7954 of 8373. Everything else billed as a CTA is an `href="#contact"` anchor - it buys a scroll, not an email. `.nav-cta` is `display: none` at <=768px, so on mobile there is no visible call to action between screen 1 and screen 15 without opening the hamburger. The mailto has no subject or body, and the address is never rendered as selectable text (only inside href attributes, verified in Hero.jsx:151 and Contact.jsx:57) - so on any machine without a configured mail client, the site's one conversion silently does nothing.
**Fix:** Render the address as copyable text beside the button; add `?subject=Project enquiry&body=` with a four-line template (what you want built / rough timeline / rough budget / deadline). Keep a compact CTA in the mobile header, or add a sticky bottom action bar once the hero scrolls out. Make each service card an anchor to a mailto whose subject names that service, capturing intent at the moment it forms.
**Suggested command:** /impeccable harden

### [P1] Hero hierarchy is inverted - the name is 3x the offer
**Why it matters:** `.hero-name` renders at 96px in brand gradient and wraps to two lines; `.hero-headline` renders at 32px capped by `max-width: 18ch`, forcing the promise into three ragged lines breaking mid-clause. A cold prospective client has no relationship with the name and it carries zero persuasive value; the promise is the entire pitch. On mobile `.hero-portrait { order: 1 }` puts the photo first, so the fold order is photo -> availability pill -> name -> promise at 60% down the fold.
**Fix:** Invert the type scale - headline to `clamp(2.25rem, 5.5vw, 3.5rem)` at `max-width: 22ch` so it sets in two clean lines; name down to ~1.5rem as a byline. Drop the order swap at <=900px so copy leads on mobile.
**Suggested command:** /impeccable typeset

### [P1] The only verifiable credential is hidden inside a 2.5-second rotator
**Why it matters:** The tagline rotator cycles four strings, three of which restate the same claim. The fourth - "Software Engineer @ Jamf" - is the only third-party verifiable credential the site owns, and the entire founder-operator positioning rests on it. It is on screen 25% of the time. Every screenshot, every social card, every glance-and-scroll is a coin flip on whether the strongest fact is visible.
**Fix:** Delete the rotator. Replace with one static line combining the binding facts: "Software Engineer at Jamf · 3 products founded · 6 apps shipped." All three are true per PRODUCT.md, all three visible simultaneously, and it makes the stats block redundant.
**Suggested command:** /impeccable distill

### [P2] Experience + Skills form a two-screen resume valley immediately before the CTA
**Why it matters:** 2.2 desktop screens (3.3 mobile) of hiring-manager content sit exactly where the peak-end rule punishes. The Jamf description runs 100 words through MongoDB schema validation, dead-letter policies, Java 25 modernization, Gradle updates, Prometheus/Grafana and ADRs. A non-technical founder reads it and concludes she is on the wrong person's site. The last emotional note before the decision is "competent backend engineer at a device-management company," not "this person will ship my app."
**Fix:** Keep Experience - Jamf is binding and it is the depth half of the positioning - but cut the description to two sentences of client-legible outcome and split the run-on title into role / company / dates. Move Experience and Skills below Contact, and fill the vacated slot with a short "How this works" block: what happens after you email, typical timeline, what you own.
**Suggested command:** /impeccable clarify

### [P2] In the Work grid, the client proof is the weakest artwork and it is buried last
**Why it matters:** The COVERS map has only three entries, so both Contract projects - the only direct evidence he delivers for other people - show no product at all, just a logo on a gradient tile. They are also last, landing 2.5 screens into a 3-screen grid. Inside a single row the eye sees a real app screenshot beside two logo tiles, which reads as a half-finished grid. CardStock and SimpleSelfBudget render with zero links and no explanation of why there is nothing to click.
**Fix:** Add covers for drivway, equine and valzcorner; reorder so one Contract item leads the grid; give the link-less cards an explicit state line ("in development" / "private"); change `.hero-scroll` href from `#about` to `#services` so the scroll cue stops skipping the client pitch.
**Suggested command:** /impeccable layout

## Persona Red Flags

**Mobile referral visitor** (arrived from a forwarded link, thumb-scrolling): `.nav-cta` is `display: none` at <=768px, so from screen 1 to screen 15 there is nothing to tap without finding the hamburger. `.hero-portrait { order: 1 }` puts a stranger's face first; the offer is at 60% of the fold. Document height at 390px is 13,545px = 16 screens; Work alone is 6.7 screens. Thumb targets under 44px: filters 37px, project links 40px, social 42px, toggle 42px. Net: she never reaches Contact.

**Keyboard / screen-reader visitor:** the six service cards are non-interactive divs, so Tab jumps from the last nav link straight to "Start a project" - the entire service menu is skipped in the tab order. Pressing a filter changes the result set with no aria-live and no rendered count; the announcement is silence while the document collapses 1,451px underneath (detector confirmed zero `[aria-live]` or `[role=status]` in the document). The envelope carrying `aria-label="Email"` is announced third in a group with LinkedIn and GitHub, presenting the conversion as a social profile.

**Non-technical founder with a $15-30k budget** (primary persona): nothing states a price band, a timeline, or a first step. The contact line tells her only that he takes a limited number of projects - his constraint, not her protection. The Jamf paragraph is unreadable to her and sits two screens before the CTA, followed by 38 acronyms. The six service cards are not clickable, so her one clear thought - "I want iOS & Mobile Apps" - cannot be carried anywhere. Net: she closes the tab and asks her network for a referral.

**Technical hiring manager** (secondary - exposes the split personality): NAV_LINKS serves him while the hero, Services and Contact serve the client, so both audiences get a diluted build. No CV download exists. The Education line gets the same gradient-text treatment as the closing CTA headline, so a degree reads as a call to action.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the site could only be one screen, what would be on it - and why isn't that screen the site? The offer, the six live App Store links and the email button fit inside one viewport. The current build spends 9.3 screens arriving at the same place.
2. What would change if the headline had to survive being read aloud by the person referring him? "I build web & iOS apps" describes activity. "I take your app idea to the App Store" is a promise with a finish line - and PRODUCT.md already names it as the client promise.
3. The absence of testimonials, logos, rates and metrics is permanent - so what is the substitute for social proof? What does the page look like if the six live store links *are* the proof: counted, dated, above the fold?
4. What actually happens after someone clicks "Get in touch"? Today: a blank compose window and silence.
